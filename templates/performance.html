Statistical Guide: Understanding Regression Evaluation Metrics

1. Mean Absolute Error (MAE): Measures the average rupee difference between predicted car valuations and actual selling prices. Random Forest achieves an average error of ₹ 1,59,985 (~ ₹ 1.60 Lakhs), outperforming Linear Regression (₹ 1,79,641) by nearly ₹ 20,000 per vehicle.

2. Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE): Squares individual residuals prior to averaging, thereby penalizing large valuation misses more heavily than small ones. Random Forest reduces RMSE from ₹ 3.84 Lakhs down to ₹ 3.68 Lakhs.

3. Critical Concept: Why R² is NOT "Prediction Accuracy": In regression, $R^2$ (Coefficient of Determination) represents the proportion of variance in the dependent variable explained by the model compared to predicting the simple mean ($\bar{y}$):

R² = 1 - [ Σ(y_i - ŷ_i)² / Σ(y_i - ȳ)² ]

Unlike classification tasks where accuracy represents the percentage of correct class labels (e.g. 95%), an $R^2$ of 0.578 does not mean the model is accurate 57.8% of the time. Instead, it indicates that 57.8% of the total variability in secondary car prices is captured by our features. Pre-owned consumer markets involve substantial unobservable variation (such as paint condition, scratch history, urgency of sale, and personal negotiation). Capturing ~58% of variance on pre-owned assets demonstrates strong predictive power without artificial overfitting.

4. Why Random Forest Won: Car depreciation follows a steep, non-linear exponential curve over time. Tree-based ensemble algorithms partition the feature space into recursive segments, allowing them to naturally capture non-linear vehicle depreciation and luxury brand premiums without rigid linearity assumptions.
